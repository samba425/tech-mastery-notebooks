# Part III — Mental Addition and Subtraction

Fast, accurate addition and subtraction are the foundation of every other calculation — including multiplication, percentages and data interpretation. This part teaches eight methods, with **more than 60 worked examples**. The goal is to do two- and three-digit sums in your head, reliably.

## Chapter 5: Mental Addition

### 5.1 Adding from left to right

:::objective
Add two- and three-digit numbers mentally by working from the largest place value first.
:::

At school we add from right to left because of carrying. In the head it is easier to go **left to right**: you hear the most important part of the answer first, and you can stop early if an estimate is enough.

[[flow: Add the hundreds | Add the tens | Add the units | Combine the three parts]]

:::why Why it works
Addition is commutative and associative, so we may add the place values in any order: 456 + 378 = (400 + 300) + (50 + 70) + (6 + 8).
:::

| # | Problem | Left-to-right working | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34 + 45 | 30 + 40 = 70; 4 + 5 = 9 | 79 |
| 2 | 58 + 27 | 50 + 20 = 70; 8 + 7 = 15; 70 + 15 | 85 |
| 3 | 76 + 69 | 70 + 60 = 130; 6 + 9 = 15 | 145 |
| 4 | 243 + 516 | 700; 50; 9 | 759 |
| 5 | 456 + 378 | 700; 120; 14 → 700 + 120 + 14 | 834 |
| 6 | 689 + 547 | 1,100; 120; 16 | 1,236 |
| 7 | 1,245 + 3,682 | 4,000; 800; 120; 7 | 4,927 |
| 8 | 8,764 + 5,398 | 13,000; 1,000; 150; 12 | 14,162 |

:::tip Running total
Keep only one number in your head: 456 + 378 → 456 + 300 = 756 → + 70 = 826 → + 8 = 834. This "add the parts of the second number one at a time" style is the most popular mental method.
:::

### 5.3 Compensation: round, then adjust

:::formula Compensation for addition
a + b = (a + rounded b) − (amount added in rounding)
:::

| # | Problem | Round and adjust | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 46 + 9 | 46 + 10 − 1 | 55 |
| 14 | 73 + 19 | 73 + 20 − 1 | 92 |
| 15 | 158 + 29 | 158 + 30 − 1 | 187 |
| 16 | 264 + 39 | 264 + 40 − 1 | 303 |
| 17 | 345 + 99 | 345 + 100 − 1 | 444 |
| 18 | 1,276 + 199 | 1,276 + 200 − 1 | 1,475 |
| 19 | 4,538 + 299 | 4,538 + 300 − 1 | 4,837 |
| 20 | 2,817 + 998 | 2,817 + 1,000 − 2 | 3,815 |
| 21 | 657 + 197 | 657 + 200 − 3 | 854 |
| 22 | 3,450 + 1,996 | 3,450 + 2,000 − 4 | 5,446 |

:::warn Adjust in the right direction
You added **too much** when rounding up, so you must **subtract** the extra. A common slip is 345 + 99 = 345 + 100 + 1 = 446 (wrong). The correct answer is 444.
:::

### 5.4 Making tens and hundreds (number bonds)

Look for pairs that make 10, 100 or 1,000.

| # | Problem | Pairing | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 7 + 8 + 3 + 2 | (7 + 3) + (8 + 2) = 10 + 10 | 20 |
| 24 | 36 + 45 + 64 | (36 + 64) + 45 = 100 + 45 | 145 |
| 25 | 125 + 380 + 875 | (125 + 875) + 380 | 1,380 |
| 26 | 47 + 28 + 53 + 72 | (47 + 53) + (28 + 72) | 200 |
| 27 | 2.6 + 3.9 + 7.4 | (2.6 + 7.4) + 3.9 = 10 + 3.9 | 13.9 |

### 5.5 Fast addition of many numbers

:::example Adding a column — "drop the tens" method
**Add 47, 86, 59, 34, 78.**
Units first: 7 + 6 = 13, + 9 = 22, + 4 = 26, + 8 = 34 → write 4, carry 3.
Tens: 4 + 8 + 5 + 3 + 7 = 27, + 3 = 30.
**Total = 304.**
:::

:::example Adding by grouping near a common value
**Add 98, 102, 97, 105, 99, 101.** All are near 100. Deviations: −2, +2, −3, +5, −1, +1 → total +2.
Sum = 6 × 100 + 2 = **602**.
:::

| # | Problem | Method | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | 23 + 45 + 67 + 89 | units 24 → 4 c2; tens 2+4+6+8+2 = 22 | 224 |
| 29 | 312 + 288 + 456 | (312 + 288) + 456 = 600 + 456 | 1,056 |
| 30 | 49 + 51 + 48 + 52 + 50 | five numbers near 50, deviations total 0 | 250 |
| 31 | 1,999 + 2,001 + 3,500 | (1,999 + 2,001) + 3,500 | 7,500 |
| 32 | 18 + 27 + 36 + 45 + 54 | pairs 18 + 54 = 72, 27 + 45 = 72, + 36 | 180 |

### 5.6 Column totals and the digit-sum check

When you add a long column of numbers (bills, marks, data tables), check the total with digit sums:

:::example Checking 4,567 + 2,986 + 1,348 = 8,901
Digit sums: 4,567 → 22 → 4; 2,986 → 25 → 7; 1,348 → 16 → 7. 4 + 7 + 7 = 18 → 9 → 0.
8,901 → 18 → 9 → 0 ✓.
:::

:::recap Mental addition
Left to right · add the parts of the second number one at a time · round and adjust · pair to make 10s and 100s · use deviations for numbers near a common value · check with digit sums.
:::

## Chapter 6: Mental Subtraction

### 6.1 Subtracting from powers of 10 (Nikhilam)

:::formula All from 9 and the last from 10
To subtract a number from 10, 100, 1,000 …: subtract each digit from 9, and the last non-zero digit from 10. Pad with leading zeros to match the number of zeros in the base.
:::

| # | Problem | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33 | 100 − 64 | 9−6 = 3, 10−4 = 6 | 36 |
| 34 | 100 − 7 | 07 → 9−0, 10−7 | 93 |
| 35 | 1,000 − 273 | 7, 2, 7 | 727 |
| 36 | 1,000 − 89 | 089 → 9, 1, 1 | 911 |
| 37 | 10,000 − 4,826 | 5, 1, 7, 4 | 5,174 |
| 38 | 10,000 − 307 | 0307 → 9, 6, 9, 3 | 9,693 |
| 39 | 1,00,000 − 36,452 | 6, 3, 5, 4, 8 | 63,548 |
| 40 | 1,000 − 640 | apply to 64 (from 100) → 36, attach 0 | 360 |

:::tip Making change
"₹500 note for a ₹347 bill" is 500 − 347 = 400 + (100 − 47) − 0 … even simpler: 500 − 347 = (499 − 347) + 1 = 152 + 1 = **₹153**. Subtracting from 499 never needs borrowing.
:::

### 6.2 Subtracting near a round number (compensation)

:::formula Compensation for subtraction
a − b = (a − rounded b) + (amount added in rounding)
:::

| # | Problem | Round and adjust | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 41 | 83 − 9 | 83 − 10 + 1 | 74 |
| 42 | 156 − 38 | 156 − 40 + 2 | 118 |
| 43 | 432 − 99 | 432 − 100 + 1 | 333 |
| 44 | 725 − 198 | 725 − 200 + 2 | 527 |
| 45 | 1,264 − 497 | 1,264 − 500 + 3 | 767 |
| 46 | 5,000 − 1,998 | 5,000 − 2,000 + 2 | 3,002 |
| 47 | 8,345 − 2,989 | 8,345 − 3,000 + 11 | 5,356 |

### 6.3 Equal adjustment (shift both numbers)

Adding or subtracting the **same amount** to both numbers does not change the difference. Make the second number round.

| # | Problem | Shift both | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 73 − 28 | (73 + 2) − (28 + 2) = 75 − 30 | 45 |
| 49 | 512 − 196 | 516 − 200 | 316 |
| 50 | 1,003 − 678 | 1,025 − 700 | 325 |
| 51 | 6,020 − 3,985 | 6,035 − 4,000 | 2,035 |
| 52 | 82.4 − 39.7 | 82.7 − 40 | 42.7 |

:::why Why equal adjustment works
On a number line, the difference is the **distance** between two points. Sliding both points the same distance keeps the gap the same.
:::

### 6.4 Counting up (the shopkeeper's method)

Find the difference by counting **up** from the smaller number to the larger, in friendly jumps.

| # | Problem | Count up | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 53 | 92 − 57 | 57 → 60 (3) → 92 (32) | 35 |
| 54 | 403 − 168 | 168 → 200 (32) → 403 (203) | 235 |
| 55 | 2,005 − 1,387 | 1,387 → 1,400 (13) → 2,000 (600) → 2,005 (5) | 618 |
| 56 | 10,000 − 6,742 | Nikhilam: 3, 2, 5, 8 | 3,258 |

### 6.5 Subtracting close numbers

When two numbers are close, the difference is small — find it by looking at the **last digits** and adjusting.

| # | Problem | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 57 | 503 − 497 | 497 → 500 (3) → 503 (3) | 6 |
| 58 | 1,002 − 989 | 989 → 1,000 (11) → 1,002 (2) | 13 |
| 59 | 7,014 − 6,958 | 6,958 → 7,000 (42) → 7,014 (14) | 56 |

### 6.6 The complement method for subtraction

To subtract b from a, you can **add the complement** of b (from the next power of 10) and then remove that power of 10.

:::example 742 − 358
Complement of 358 from 1,000 is 642 (all from 9, last from 10). 742 + 642 = 1,384. Remove 1,000 → **384**.
:::

This is how calculators and computers subtract internally, and it turns every subtraction into an addition.

| # | Problem | Complement working | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 623 − 287 | 623 + 713 = 1,336 → remove 1,000 | 336 |
| 61 | 5,104 − 2,768 | 5,104 + 7,232 = 12,336 → remove 10,000 | 2,336 |

### 6.7 Cross-checking results

:::formula Three checks for subtraction
1. **Add back:** answer + number subtracted = original number.
2. **Digit sums:** digit sum of (a − b) equals digit sum of a minus digit sum of b (add 9 if negative).
3. **Estimate:** 8,345 − 2,989 ≈ 8,300 − 3,000 = 5,300; the answer 5,356 is close ✓.
:::

:::warn Mistakes to watch for
- Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in every column (writing 2 for "7 minus 9") — this is the most common error in written subtraction.
- Forgetting the padding zero in Nikhilam: 1,000 − 89 is 911, not 11.
:::

## Chapter 7: Mental Arithmetic Games for Children

These games build the same skills in a playful way. Play for 5–10 minutes a day. Part X has a full children's course.

:::parent How to play
Use real objects at first (coins, buttons, blocks). Let the child explain their thinking aloud. Praise strategies ("you made a ten — smart!") more than speed.
:::

1. **Make Ten Snap.** Shuffle cards 1–9. Turn two over; if they make 10 (3 + 7, 4 + 6 …), shout "Ten!" and keep the pair.
2. **Bus Stop.** "There are 12 people on the bus. 5 get off, 3 get on. How many now?" Change numbers each round.
3. **Shop Keeper.** Give ₹50 in toy notes; buy items priced ₹18, ₹23, ₹37. The child counts up to give change.
4. **Number-line Jumps.** Draw a line from 0 to 30 on the floor with chalk. Call out "start at 8, jump forward 7" — the child jumps and calls the landing number.
5. **Doubles Race.** Call a number; the child doubles it. Then "near doubles": 7 + 8 is double 7 plus 1.
6. **Target 100.** Two players take turns adding 1–10 to a running total. Whoever says exactly 100 wins. (Hidden lesson: work backwards from 100 in steps of 11.)

:::recap Part III summary
- Add left to right, one part at a time.
- Round and adjust: add 99 = add 100, take 1.
- Subtract from 10ⁿ: all from 9, last from 10.
- Shift both numbers to make subtraction easy.
- Count up for differences; use complements to turn subtraction into addition.
- Always check: add back, digit sums, estimate.
:::
