# Part X — Mental Maths for Children (Ages 5–12)

This part is written for **parents and teachers**, with child-facing pages they can read together. It builds number sense first and shortcuts later. A child who understands *why* 9 + 6 = 15 will learn the shortcuts easily; a child who only memorises tricks will forget them.

:::parent Five rules for parents
1. **Short and daily beats long and weekly.** 10–15 minutes a day is ideal.
2. **Use objects before symbols.** Beads, coins, blocks and fingers come before written sums.
3. **Praise the method, not just the answer.** "I like how you made a ten first!"
4. **Never rush or time young children.** Speed comes from understanding, not pressure.
5. **Stop while it is still fun.** End every session with a success.
:::

:::warn Avoid comparison and anxiety
Do not compare your child with siblings or classmates. Maths anxiety, once it starts, is hard to undo. Mistakes are a normal part of learning. Say "Let's check it together", not "Wrong!"
:::

## Chapter 42: Level 1 — Ages 5 to 7 (Number Sense)

### 42.1 Counting and number recognition

- Count real objects, touching each one once.
- Count forwards and backwards from any number (from 7 back to 0).
- Recognise small groups without counting (dice patterns, fingers). This skill is called **subitising**.

[[diagram:objects 3 4 apple]]

*"How many apples are in each basket? How many baskets? How many altogether?"* (3 groups of 4 make 12.)

### 42.2 Ten frames and number bonds

[[diagram:ten_frame 7]]

A ten frame shows how far a number is from ten. 7 dots leave **3 empty spaces**, so 7 and 3 make 10. This is the beginning of *Nikhilam* thinking: "how far from 10?"

:::memory Number bonds of 10 — the most important facts at this level
1 + 9 · 2 + 8 · 3 + 7 · 4 + 6 · 5 + 5. Sing them, clap them, play "snap" with them.
:::

### 42.3 Adding and subtracting within 20

[[diagram:number_line 0 20 8>13 13>16]]

:::tip Make a ten (child version)
8 + 5: "8 needs 2 to make 10. Take 2 from the 5. Now 10 + 3 = 13."
:::

:::example Child-friendly practice
- 9 + 4: 9 needs 1 → 10 + 3 = **13**
- 7 + 6: 7 needs 3 → 10 + 3 = **13**
- 15 − 7: 15 − 5 = 10, then 2 more → **8**
- 12 − 9: from 9 up to 12 is **3** (count up!)
:::

### 42.4 Doubles and halves

Doubles: 1 + 1, 2 + 2, … 10 + 10. **Near doubles**: 6 + 7 is double 6 plus 1, which gives **13**.

### 42.5 Patterns and shapes

- Continue the pattern: 2, 4, 6, 8, __ (10).
- Spot shapes around the house: circles, squares, triangles, rectangles.

:::parent Level 1 games
- **Bond snap:** say a number; the child says its partner to 10.
- **Shop:** price toys at ₹1–₹10 and pay with real coins.
- **Stairs counting:** count up and down stairs in 1s and 2s.
- **Dice race:** roll two dice and add; the first to 50 wins.
:::

## Chapter 43: Level 2 — Ages 8 to 10 (Fluency)

### 43.1 Place value and bigger numbers

Use bundles of ten sticks and a place-value chart (Chapter 1). Read 4,07,315 aloud as "four lakh seven thousand three hundred fifteen".

### 43.2 Mental addition and subtraction to 1,000

- Add left to right: 346 + 237 = 500 + 70 + 13 = **583**.
- Round and adjust: 198 + 456 = 200 + 456 − 2 = **654**.
- Subtract from 100 and 1,000 with **"all from 9 and the last from 10"**: 1,000 − 368 = **632**.

### 43.3 Times tables — understanding before memorising

[[diagram:array 4 6]]

An array shows that 4 × 6 and 6 × 4 are the same rectangle turned around, so the child learns **half** as many facts.

:::tip Learn tables in this order
×2, ×10, ×5 (half of ×10), ×4 (double ×2), ×9 (×10 minus one group), ×3, ×6 (double ×3), ×8 (double ×4), ×7 last. By then, only 7 × 7 is genuinely new.
:::

:::tip The 9 times table finger trick
Hold up 10 fingers. For 9 × 4, fold down the 4th finger: 3 fingers on the left, 6 on the right → **36**. Works for 9 × 1 to 9 × 10.
:::

### 43.4 First Vedic shortcuts

- **×11** for 2-digit numbers: for 34 × 11, write 3, then 3 + 4 = 7 in the middle, then 4 → **374**.
- **Squares ending in 5**: 35² = (3 × 4) | 25 = **1,225**.
- **×5**: halve and multiply by 10: 46 × 5 = **230**.

### 43.5 Fractions with pictures

[[diagram:fraction_bar 3 4]]

"Three of the four equal parts are coloured." Always draw before writing fractions. Pizza, roti and chocolate bars are excellent models.

### 43.6 Money and time

:::example Money and time
- A pen costs ₹18 and a notebook ₹35. Total **₹53**. Change from ₹100: **₹47**.
- School starts at 8:45 and ends at 2:15. That is **5 hours 30 minutes**.
:::

:::parent Level 2 games
- **Bus stop maths:** add the digits of number plates.
- **Price guess:** at the shop, estimate the bill before paying.
- **Table bingo:** call out products; the child marks the matching multiplication.
- **Magic squares:** see Chapter 44.
:::

## Chapter 44: Level 3 — Ages 11 to 12 (Confidence and Problem Solving)

### 44.1 Two-digit multiplication

Teach both the standard column method and **vertically and crosswise** (Chapter 10). Let the child choose the one they prefer, and check with the other.

### 44.2 Base methods near 10 and 100

97 × 96: both are below 100 by 3 and 4. 97 − 4 = 93; 3 × 4 = 12 → **9,312**.

### 44.3 Fractions, decimals and percentages

Memorise the halves, quarters, fifths and eighths chart (Chapter 3). 25% of 60 is a quarter of 60, which is **15**.

### 44.4 Puzzles and logic

[[diagram:magic_square]]

Every row, column and diagonal adds to **15**. Challenge: make a magic square with 2 to 10 (each line will add to 18).

### 44.5 Bar models for word problems

[[diagram:bar_model 3 2 ₹500]]

*"Riya and Arjun share ₹500 in the ratio 3 : 2."* Five equal parts → one part ₹100 → Riya **₹300**, Arjun **₹200**.

:::recap What each level should achieve
- **Level 1:** count confidently, bonds of 10, add and subtract within 20.
- **Level 2:** place value to lakhs, mental add/subtract to 1,000, all tables to 10 × 10, first shortcuts.
- **Level 3:** 2-digit multiplication two ways, base methods, fraction–percentage chart, bar models.
:::

## Chapter 45: Daily Plans, Rewards and Progress

### 45.1 A 15-minute daily routine

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Warm-up game (bonds, doubles, or tables) |
| 3–8 | One new idea with objects or a picture |
| 8–12 | Practice (5–8 questions) |
| 12–15 | A puzzle or game; finish on a success |

### 45.2 A 4-week starter plan (Level 2)

| Week | Focus | Goal by the end of the week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bonds of 10 and 100; make-a-ten | 20 bond questions in 2 minutes, no stress |
| 2 | Left-to-right addition; round and adjust | add two 3-digit numbers mentally |
| 3 | Tables ×2, ×5, ×10, ×4, ×9 | answer any of these in 3 seconds |
| 4 | ×11 and squares ending in 5 | explain *why* the trick works using an array |

### 45.3 Rewards that work

- Sticker charts for **effort** (practised today), not only for speed.
- A "maths champion" certificate after every completed week.
- Let the child **teach** a trick to a family member. Teaching is the best reward and the best revision.
