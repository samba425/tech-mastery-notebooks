# Welcome — How to use this book

## Before You Begin

### Who this book is for

This book starts at **zero**. If you can count to ten, you can begin on page one. By the end you will be able to calculate quickly in your head, check your own answers, and solve the arithmetic and aptitude questions that appear in school tests and in competitive examinations such as SSC, Banking, Railways (RRB NTPC, RRB JE), state-level exams and the CSAT paper.

The book has separate paths for:

- **Children (ages 5–12)** — Part X is a gentle, game-based course. Parents and teachers will find instructions in the purple boxes.
- **School students** — Parts I–VII build strong calculation and algebra skills.
- **Adults** who want speed and confidence with numbers — Parts III, IV, VI and IX.
- **Government-exam aspirants** — Parts VIII, XI, XII and XIII, plus the roadmap in Part XIV.

Every major technique follows the same pattern, so you always know where to look:

### How to study

1. Read the explanation slowly. Say the steps aloud.
2. Cover the solution of each worked example and try it yourself first.
3. Do the practice questions **on paper** before looking at the answer key.
4. Check every answer. If you got it wrong, find the exact step where you went wrong.
5. Revise with the quick-revision boxes the next day, and again after a week.

:::tip Understanding first, speed second
Speed comes naturally after accuracy. A fast wrong answer scores nothing — and in exams with negative marking it costs marks. Practise slowly and correctly; speed will follow.
:::

## What is Vedic Mathematics?

### The short answer

**Vedic Mathematics** is the name given to a collection of mental calculation techniques published by **Sri Bharati Krishna Tirthaji** (1884–1960), a scholar and the Shankaracharya of Puri. His book *Vedic Mathematics* was published in 1965, after his death. It presents the methods through **16 sutras** (short "word-formulae") and **13 sub-sutras**.

Examples of what these methods do:

- Multiply numbers close to 100 or 1,000 in one line (*Nikhilam*).
- Multiply any two numbers by a neat "vertically and crosswise" pattern (*Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam*).
- Square numbers ending in 5 instantly (*Ekadhikena Purvena*).
- Divide by 9, 99, or by numbers such as 12 and 112 with very little writing.
- Solve some special equations by inspection.

:::history What is known, and what is claimed
Tirthaji stated that the sutras came from an appendix (*parishishta*) of the *Atharvaveda*. Historians and Sanskrit scholars have **not found these sutras in any known Vedic text**, and many regard them as Tirthaji's own composition, written in Sanskrit style. This book therefore uses the phrase "traditional sutras" to mean *the sutras as presented in Tirthaji's book* — not a claim about ancient origin.

What is beyond dispute is that **the techniques themselves are correct mathematics**. Each one can be proved with ordinary algebra, and this book shows you the proof (the "Why it works" boxes). India also has a genuine and rich mathematical history — Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara II, the Kerala school — that is separate from this particular system.
:::

### What Vedic methods are — and are not

| Vedic methods are… | Vedic methods are not… |
|---|---|
| Clever, correct shortcuts for particular kinds of numbers | A replacement for understanding place value, fractions and algebra |
| Very fast when the numbers suit the method (near a base, ending in 5, etc.) | Faster for *every* calculation |
| A great way to build number sense and confidence | Magic — every step can be explained |
| Useful for checking answers in a second way | Proof of any historical claim |

:::warn Do not force a shortcut
If the numbers do not fit a method, use the standard method. Choosing the right method is itself a skill. Throughout this book, every technique has a "When **not** to use it" section.
:::

### Notation used in this book

- Numbers are written with **Indian grouping**: 1,00,000 is one lakh; 1,00,00,000 is one crore. Part I explains how this compares with the international system (100,000 and 10,000,000).
- The **vertical bar** `|` separates the left and right parts of a Vedic answer. For example `93|12` means 9312.
- A **minus sign** is written −. Negative deviations are shown in brackets, e.g. (−3).
- A **bar over a digit** (vinculum), such as 1̅, means "minus 1" in that place. It is introduced in Part II.
- "≈" means *approximately equal to*. Answers with ≈ are estimates.
- Money is written in rupees, e.g. ₹2,450.

### About the practice questions

All practice questions in this book are **original**. They were produced by a program that also computes each answer, and every answer was checked a second time by an independent calculation (see the `tests/` folder in the source files). Where a topic is commonly tested in SSC, Banking or Railway exams this is mentioned, but **no question is presented as an official previous-year question**.
