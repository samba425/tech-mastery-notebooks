# Part XI — Vedic Maths for Government Exams

This part shows how to apply mental maths to the quantitative sections of Indian competitive exams, such as SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO), Banking (IBPS and SBI PO and Clerk, RRB), Railways (RRB NTPC and Group D), state PSCs, police recruitment, and defence entrance tests.

:::warn About exam patterns and questions
- Exam patterns, syllabi, marks and negative marking **change from year to year**. Always read the **official notification** for your exam.
- Every question in this book is an **original question written in the style of** these exams. None is presented as a previous-year question.
- No method can guarantee selection. Speed comes from understanding plus regular, timed practice.
:::

## Chapter 46: How Quantitative Sections Work

### 46.1 Common question families

| Family | Typical in | Most useful skills |
|---|---|---|
| Simplification and approximation | Banking prelims, SSC, Railways | BODMAS, fraction chart, squares, rounding |
| Number series | Banking | differences, squares/cubes, ×n ± k patterns |
| Quadratic comparison | Banking | factorising quadratics quickly |
| Arithmetic word problems | All | percentages, ratio, profit, interest, work, speed |
| Data interpretation (DI) | Banking mains, SSC CGL | percentages, ratios, averages, estimation |
| Number system, algebra, geometry, trigonometry | SSC CGL, CPO | identities, remainders, mensuration formulas |

### 46.2 Where Vedic methods genuinely help

- **Calculation-heavy steps**: multiplying 2–3-digit numbers, squaring, dividing by numbers near a base.
- **Simplification**: × 11, × 5/25/125, near-base products, squares of numbers near 50/100.
- **Checking**: digit-sum and units-digit checks to eliminate options.
- **DI**: percentage ↔ fraction conversions and quick estimation.

### 46.3 Where they do not help much

- Understanding the word problem (that needs reading and modelling).
- Geometry and trigonometry (mostly formulas and properties).
- Reasoning sections.

:::when Choosing method versus speed
Use a shortcut only if you can recognise it **within 2–3 seconds**. Otherwise do the standard method you trust. A wrong fast answer costs marks if there is negative marking.
:::

## Chapter 47: Simplification and Approximation

### 47.1 Simplification (exact)

:::example
**25% of 480 + 3/4 of 160 − √196 = ?**
25% of 480 is 120; 3/4 of 160 is 120; √196 is 14. Answer: 120 + 120 − 14 = **226**.
:::

:::example
**(48 × 52) ÷ 13 + 15² = ?**
48 × 52 = 2,500 − 4 = 2,496 (difference of squares). 2,496 ÷ 13 = 192. 15² = 225. Answer: **417**.
:::

### 47.2 Approximation questions

Answer options are usually far apart. Round **each** number to the nearest convenient value.

:::approx
**299.8 ÷ 5.02 + 11.97² ≈ ?** Use 300 ÷ 5 = 60 and 12² = 144 → **≈ 204**.
:::

:::warn Do not over-round
Rounding 11.97 to 10 instead of 12 changes 144 into 100, enough to pick the wrong option. Round to the **nearest** easy number, not the nearest ten.
:::

## Chapter 48: Number Series and Quadratic Comparison

### 48.1 Number series: a checklist

1. Find the differences. Are they constant, increasing steadily, or doubling?
2. Try ×2, ×3 with ± a constant.
3. Look for squares/cubes (n², n² ± 1, n³).
4. Look for two interleaved series (odd and even positions).

:::example Number series
- 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ? → each term × 2 + 1 → **127**.
- 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? → n(n + 1): 1·2, 2·3, … → **42**.
- 5, 11, 23, 47, ? → × 2 + 1 → **95**.
- 1, 8, 27, 64, ? → cubes → **125**.
- 120, 60, 20, 5, ? → ÷ 2, ÷ 3, ÷ 4, ÷ 5 → **1**.
:::

### 48.2 Quadratic comparison

:::example
I: x² − 7x + 12 = 0 → x = 3 or 4. II: y² − 9y + 20 = 0 → y = 4 or 5.
Compare every pair: 3 < 4, 3 < 5, 4 = 4, 4 < 5 → **x ≤ y**.
:::

:::tip Sign shortcut for quadratic roots
For x² + bx + c = 0: if b and c are both positive, both roots are negative. If b is negative and c positive, both roots are positive. If c is negative, the roots have opposite signs.
:::

## Chapter 49: Data Interpretation

[[diagram:compare_bars A=120 B=150 C=90 D=180 E=160]]

:::example Sales (₹ lakh) of five companies, shown above
1. Average sales = (120 + 150 + 90 + 180 + 160) ÷ 5 = 700 ÷ 5 = **140**.
2. By what percentage are D's sales more than C's? (180 − 90)/90 × 100 = **100%**.
3. Ratio of B to E = 150 : 160 = **15 : 16**.
4. C's share of the total = 90/700 ≈ **12.9%** (≈ 1/8, since 1/8 = 12.5%).
:::

### 49.1 A table-based set

Production of a factory (thousand units):

| Year | Cars | Bikes |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 40 | 120 |
| 2022 | 45 | 150 |
| 2023 | 54 | 135 |
| 2024 | 60 | 162 |

:::example Questions on the table
1. Percentage increase in car production from 2021 to 2024: (60 − 40)/40 × 100 = **50%**.
2. Percentage change in bike production from 2022 to 2023: (135 − 150)/150 × 100 = **−10%** (a 10% fall).
3. Average bike production: 567 ÷ 4 = **141.75** thousand.
4. Ratio of total production in 2023 to 2021: 189 : 160.
5. In which year did car production grow fastest? Growth was 5/40 = 12.5% in 2022, 9/45 = **20% in 2023**, and 6/54 ≈ 11.1% in 2024. **Answer: 2023.**
:::

:::warn Largest increase vs largest percentage increase
The biggest jump in *units* and the biggest jump in *percentage* can happen in different years. Read which one the question asks for.
:::

:::tip DI routine
Read the title, units and scale **first**. Convert percentages into fractions (Chapter 3). Estimate before you calculate, and calculate exactly only when two options are close.
:::

## Chapter 50: Speed Strategy, Traps and Time Management

### 50.1 The two-pass method

1. **Pass 1:** answer every question you can do in under 45 seconds. Mark the rest.
2. **Pass 2:** return to marked questions in order of confidence.
3. **Never** spend more than about 2 minutes on one question in a speed test.

### 50.2 Option elimination

- **Units digit:** 47 × 83 must end in 1.
- **Digital root:** the digital root of the answer must match the digital root of the calculation.
- **Size:** an estimate removes options of the wrong magnitude.
- **Parity and divisibility:** an answer to "how many students" must be a whole number.

### 50.3 Ten common traps

| Trap | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| Profit % taken on SP instead of CP | Profit % is on CP unless stated |
| Averaging speeds directly | Use total distance ÷ total time |
| Forgetting 2ab in (a + b)² | Draw the area square mentally |
| Successive % treated as additive | Use a + b + ab/100 |
| Wrong units (km/h vs m/s) | Convert first, × 5/18 |
| Ignoring "at least" | Use 1 − P(none) |
| Counting overlap twice | Subtract P(A and B) |
| Reading "less than" backwards | Write the equation in words first |
| Leap-year error in calendars | Century years must be divisible by 400 |
| Over-rounding in approximation | Round to the nearest easy number |

### 50.4 Accuracy versus speed

:::why Why accuracy comes first
With negative marking, a 90%-accurate candidate who attempts 40 questions often scores more than a 60%-accurate candidate who attempts 60. Build accuracy first, then speed.
:::
